House District 14 · Profile

Rep. Karianne Lisonbee

House Judiciary and House Education committees · Criminal Justice Appropriations Subcommittee

Two nonprofit board seats listed on her 2025 conflict of interest form are not on her 2026 form, and state business records list her on two nonprofit boards. Ten 2026 bills, seven signed. Eight years of campaign money, with her largest payee listed mostly as "Campaign Expense."

The Weber County Hive Davis County (Syracuse area) In office since January 1, 2017

Posted Nov. 26, 2026 · Updated Nov. 26, 2026

In plain words

Karianne Lisonbee has represented part of Davis County in the Utah House since 2017. She did not run for her House seat again in 2026. She ran for Congress instead and lost the Republican primary to Rep. Blake Moore in June. This page shows what her own forms, bills and campaign reports say, so readers can look at her record as her House term closes.

The Basics

OfficeUtah House District 14, Davis County. Republican. In office since January 1, 2017 (her House page).

What the records show

Wasatch International Adoption Agency and Open Doors are listed on her 2025 form and not on her 2026 form. The state's business record for Wasatch International Adoptions, renewed April 9, 2026, still listed her as a director three months after her 2026 form was filed.

Family Connection Center, Inc. does not appear on her 2025 or 2026 form. Item 5A of the form asks for any board seat, "paid or unpaid," and she marked it N/A both years.

The Sept. 19, 2024 date is when her entry in the business record was last updated, not when she joined that board. Neither the forms nor the business records explain the differences between them.

Three Ways to Look at It

1. The auditor's look: do the records line up?

  • Reports were filed every year. Five reports in each election year (2018, 2020, 2022, 2024) and a Year End report in each other year (2019, 2021, 2023, 2025). A conflict of interest form was filed each January (Jan. 2, 2024; Jan. 9, 2025; Jan. 5, 2026).
  • The totals check out. Our 2022, 2023 and 2025 totals from October match the totals we pulled again on Nov. 26. Money she moved between her own accounts ($24,689.07) is counted once, not twice.
  • Where the records don't line up. Seventeen lines name "Smith's Food and Drug" as the payee while the purpose says mileage paid back to her. Five payments to one person ($22,205.31) give only "Campaign Expense" as the purpose. Her forms and the state business records list different boards.

2. The collective rubric: is it good for everyone?

See the grade cards above. In short: Her child-welfare bills add ways to complain and yearly reports. Her prison bills add costs, and those costs are estimated in public. Her campaign reports show who gave and who was paid, but not always what was bought.

3. The Legislature's view: what the law asks, and how lawmakers voted

  • The form follows state law. Utah Code 20A-11-1604 sets what goes on the form. Item 5A asks for any board seat, "paid or unpaid."
  • Most of her bills passed with wide support. Seven of ten were signed. Five passed with one "no" vote or none. H.B. 48 was the most divided (56-14 and 20-7).
  • Three did not pass. H.B. 480 was held twice on the last night and got no Senate vote. H.B. 234 failed on the House floor. H.B. 435 was never sent to a committee.
  • The cost numbers come from the Legislature itself. The fiscal notes above are written by the Legislature's own budget office.
