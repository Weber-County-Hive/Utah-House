In plain words

Schultz has represented Utah House District 12 (Hooper / West Weber and west Davis). He began serving January 1, 2015. This page shows what his own forms, bills and campaign reports say.

Bank ties on his forms
  • 2015–2017: His conflict of interest forms from 2015, 2016, 2017 and June 2017 list "FNB Bancorp & Subsidiary" as a company he held shares in, and "First National Bank" as a board seat (Board of Directors, banking).
  • 2019: Glacier Bancorp's SEC filings show it bought FNB Bancorp of Layton on April 30, 2019. It paid almost all of the price in Glacier stock: $87.2 million in shares and about $4,000 in cash.
  • 2022–2026: Every form he filed on the state site in these years (Jan. 10, 2022; Jan. 13, 2023; Jan. 2 and Feb. 9, 2024; Jan. 7 and Jan. 8, 2025; Jan. 6, 2026) lists Glacier Bank Inc as an income source and a stock holding, each $5,000 or more. None lists a board seat.
  • What the records do not show: The forms do not say how or when he got his Glacier stock, or how much he holds. The names match; the records in hand do not show that his Glacier stock came from the FNB Bancorp sale. His forms for 2018 through 2021 were not found on the state disclosure site (its forms for him start in 2022) or among the Legislature's posted PDFs (which stop at June 2017).

How these were read: the 2015–2017 forms are PDF files posted by the Legislature (le.utah.gov), read by text extraction and matched line by line on all four forms, Nov. 26, 2026. The 2022–2026 forms were opened on disclosures.utah.gov and read from the rendered page text, Nov. 26, 2026. The Glacier purchase figures are from Glacier Bancorp's 2019 SEC filings.

Graded Against the Rubric

Every finding is graded the same five ways used across Hive coverage: Power, Transparency, Financial accountability, Environmental impact and Community impact.

Transparency — His 2025 and 2026 forms list the same entries. Item 8A, for any other possible conflict, is blank.
Financial accountability — The form lists sources, not amounts.
Environmental impact — Not applicable.
Community impact — His companies build homes and apartments. Housing and land-use bills come before the House he leads.
What could have been done differentlyItem 8A is optional. A note there tying his companies to the subjects the House votes on would have put them side by side for readers.

Three Ways to Look at It

1. The auditor's look: do the records line up?

  • Reports were filed for each year shown (2018–2026). The totals come from one pull on Nov. 26, 2026, with duplicate and deleted lines removed.
  • "Democracy Engine" is listed as a donor without the individual givers.
  • Transfers between his own accounts ($73,496.41 over 2018, 2020 and 2022) are counted once.
  • $47,057.90 is labeled only "Campaign Expense."

2. The collective rubric: is it good for everyone?

See the grade cards above. Each card ends with what could have been done differently, based only on what the record leaves open.

3. The Legislature's view: what the law asks, and how lawmakers voted

  • The form follows state law. Utah Code 20A-11-1604 sets what goes on the conflict of interest form. It asks for sources, not amounts, and Item 8A is optional.
  • Campaign reports follow state law. Each line shows a date, name, amount and a purpose label chosen by the campaign.
  • Utah Code 20A-11-1604 sets what goes on the form. It does not ask for amounts.
  • His one 2026 bill, H.B. 274, passed.
